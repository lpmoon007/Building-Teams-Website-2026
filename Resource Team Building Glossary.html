Team building glossary

Reference28 terms, plainly defined
In short

A plain-English reference to the terms you’ll hear when planning corporate team building — from experiential learning and the debrief to psychological safety, give-back builds, Tuckman’s stages and team building ROI. Definitions link through to deeper guides where we have them.

Team building
Structured activities and facilitation designed to improve how a group works together — trust, communication, collaboration and shared purpose. Effective team building changes behavior, not just mood. See our team building services.
Corporate team building
Team building run for a company’s employees — from small teams to thousands — usually tied to a business goal such as collaboration, culture, onboarding or a kickoff.
Give-back build (charity team building)
A team building activity where the work itself is charitable: teams build real goods such as bikes, skateboards or shoes that are donated to people in need, so team development and community impact happen at once. See charity team building.
Experiential learning
Learning by doing and then reflecting. Participants have a hands-on experience, debrief what happened, and apply the insight — the basis of Kolb’s learning cycle. See experiential learning.
Debrief
The facilitated discussion after an activity that connects what just happened to real work. The debrief, not the activity, is where most of the learning lands.
Facilitation
The skilled guidance of a group through an activity and its debrief so the experience produces insight and behavior change rather than just entertainment. See our facilitation workshop.
Psychological safety
A shared belief that it’s safe to speak up, ask questions, admit mistakes and take interpersonal risks without fear of blame — a foundation of high-performing teams. See psychological safety.
Tuckman’s stages
Bruce Tuckman’s model of team development — forming, storming, norming, performing (and later adjourning) — describing how a new group becomes a real team. See team building for new teams.
Icebreaker
A short, low-stakes activity that helps people relax and start interacting at the beginning of a meeting or event. See icebreakers for meetings.
Sales kickoff (SKO)
An annual meeting that brings a company’s sales organization together to launch the fiscal year — aligning on strategy, training on products and motivating the team. See sales kickoff team building.
General session
The main plenary at a conference or meeting where the whole audience gathers at once — the natural slot for a large-group team building moment.
Offsite / retreat
A meeting held away from the usual workplace to focus a team on strategy, alignment or connection, often with team building built in.
Large group team building
Team building engineered to scale so hundreds or thousands of people participate at once, with no rotations and no spectators. See large group team building.
Conference team building
A team building activity run as part of a conference, typically staged in the general session for the whole audience. See conference team building.
DISC assessment
A behavioral self-assessment that sorts people into Dominance, Influence, Steadiness and Conscientiousness styles, used to improve communication and teamwork. See DISC for teams.
Leadership simulation
An exercise that drops leaders into a realistic scenario to reveal how they actually decide, communicate and operate together under pressure. See what is a leadership simulation.
Post-merger integration
The work of combining two organizations after a merger or acquisition — including the human side of turning two teams into one. See post-merger team building.
Employee engagement
The degree to which employees feel committed to and energized by their work and organization. Low engagement is linked to lower performance and higher turnover. See the State of Team Building.
CSR / ESG (in team building)
Corporate social responsibility and environmental, social and governance goals. A give-back build produces documented community impact that can support CSR and ESG reporting. See measurement & impact.
Team building ROI
The return on a team building investment, measured by tying an event to a specific business outcome and following behavior afterward — not by how much people enjoyed it. See how to measure team building ROI.
Behavior-change measurement
Assessing whether an event actually changed how people work — typically by capturing a baseline, tying the event to a target behavior, and following up over a set period such as 30 days. See measurable team building.
Kolb’s experiential learning cycle
David Kolb’s four-stage model of learning from experience: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualization and active experimentation.
Ropes course
A traditional outdoor team building format using physical challenges — high or low elements — to build trust and communication.
Scavenger hunt
A timed, clue-based team activity in which groups complete challenges around a venue or city to build collaboration and energy. See the Ultimate Hunt.
Team charter
A short written agreement a team creates that defines its purpose, roles, goals and working norms.
Team norms
The explicit or unspoken rules for how a team communicates, makes decisions and handles conflict.
Group vs. team
A group is a set of people who share a space or a manager; a team shares a goal and depends on one another to reach it. Team building is what turns a group into a team.
The reveal
In a give-back build, the moment the beneficiaries — often children — arrive to receive what the team built. It’s the emotional peak that makes the lesson stick.
